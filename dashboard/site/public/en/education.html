What do we educate for, and how AI-exposed is the job?

Each institution awards degrees in particular fields. Each degree typically leads to particular occupations. And each occupation has an AI exposure, the same one the index uses. This page links the three. The result is a measure of how AI-exposed the jobs of each institution's graduates usually are. The first version covers six institutions: UiO, UiB, NTNU, UiS, NHH and BI.

Summary for the impatient reader
  • The business schools are on top. NHH and BI send graduates into accounting, finance, real estate and case handling. Those are occupations high on the exposure scale.
  • The comprehensive universities sit close together. UiO, UiB and NTNU have about the same mean, because teachers, health workers and engineers pull down where lawyers and economists pull up.
  • The measure sets the distance. With Mouchel et al. (2026) as the measure, the gap between the business schools and the universities widens, because that measure lifts auditing, financial analysis and case handling most.

1 · Mean exposure by institution

Graduate-weighted mean of the occupational exposure of the jobs graduates typically go to, for the latest graduating cohort. Two measures are shown: Eloundou et al. (2024), the default on the index page, and Mouchel et al. (2026). Both are scaled 0 to 1.

The mean is over everyone employed in Norway with the same degrees, not the institution's own graduates. A business graduate from BI and one from NHH get the same occupational profile when the programmes share an education code. See method.

5 · Where do they work?

Method

Three sources are linked. DBH (HK-dir) gives graduates per study programme with an education code (NUS2000). utdanning.no gives, from Statistics Norway's register data, how many people with each education code work in each occupation (STYRK-08). Occupational exposure is the same as on the index page. The exposure of a degree is the person-weighted mean over the occupations graduates actually hold. The exposure of an institution is the graduate-weighted mean over its programmes.

Caveats
  1. Occupational profiles are national per education code, not per institution. That is the main simplification.
  2. The register link is from November 2024 and covers residents aged 20 to 70. Graduate counts are for 2025.
  3. DBH suppresses small cells. Programme rows sum slightly below institution totals.
  4. Graduates are qualifications awarded, not persons.
  5. Both exposure measures are theoretical task assessments, not observed use.
  6. Recent graduates rest on a column in utdanning.no that is not documented in the API. We read it as completed one to three years ago.
Next step

The first version covers six institutions. The pipeline can be run for every institution with accredited programmes in DBH, probably 30 to 40. Small institutions will get thinner numbers because DBH suppresses small cells. The extension is planned.

Download data

The institution table has one row per institution with graduate counts, mean exposure under both measures, quintile shares and the level and field mix. The data may be used freely with attribution to Arbeidsmarkedet (kiindeksen.no).
