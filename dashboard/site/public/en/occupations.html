Automation or augmentation, occupation by occupation

AI can be used in two ways on a job. Either the model gets a task and delivers an answer. Or the person and the model work together, with questions, drafts and checks along the way. Anthropic calls the first automation and the second augmentation. This page shows the split for 350 Norwegian occupations, built on how Claude is actually used on the tasks in each occupation. Chat and API are shown separately, and the numbers exist for four points in time from December 2024 to February 2026.

Summary for the impatient reader
  • Chat is split down the middle, the API is automation. In chat about half of conversations are automation. In the API, where developers and agents call the model, nearly everything is direct delegation.
  • The ranking of occupations is not fixed. The order by automation share moves a lot from sample to sample. It is most stable for occupations with many conversations.
  • This is global use, not Norwegian. The tasks are O*NET tasks, and the conversations come from the whole world. The link to Norwegian occupations runs through SOC and ISCO.

      Method

      The source is the Anthropic Economic Index. Anthropic classifies a sample of conversations by which O*NET task they concern, and by how the person and the model interact. The task × type table has been published for four samples. The first is the sample in Handa et al. (2025), Claude.ai conversations from December 2024 to January 2025. The next three are one week each: August 2025, November 2025 and February 2026. From August 2025 the table also exists for the API.

      The five interaction types

      Directive: the person asks for a finished result and gets it. Feedback loop: the model does the work, the person only relays feedback from the environment, such as an error message. Task iteration: the person and the model build the result together over several rounds. Validation: the person uses the model to check their own work. Learning: the person wants to understand something, not get something done.

      Anthropic counts the first two as automation and the last three as augmentation. Conversations that could not be classified are left out of the denominator. Note that the index page and the working paper use a stricter rule for the usage groups: only directive use counts as automation there, and the numbers come from the first sample. The directive-only share is in the download file.

      From O*NET task to Norwegian occupation

      Each task belongs to one or more US SOC occupations. Conversations are split equally between them. SOC goes to ISCO-08 with the BLS crosswalk, and the ISCO code is the STYRK-08 code when it exists in the Norwegian standard. The share for a STYRK occupation is the mean over the contributing SOC occupations. This is exactly the chain used for the Handa measure on the index page. It yields 300 to 320 occupations per sample. Occupations with no Claude use on any of their tasks drop out.

      Figure 4 shows the tasks behind one occupation: the 15 O*NET tasks with most use among the SOC occupations the code maps to, with at least ten classified conversations.

      Caveats
      1. The conversations are global. They say how Claude is used on the occupation's tasks, not how Norwegian workers use AI.
      2. Claude only. OpenAI publishes no comparable table.
      3. The shares are of conversations, not of working time. An occupation where Claude is rarely used can get a high automation share from few conversations. The number of classified conversations is in the tooltip.
      4. The ranking moves. Between the August 2025 and February 2026 samples the rank correlation in chat is about 0.7. Against the Handa sample it is lower, about 0.3, and about 0.6 for occupations with at least a thousand conversations.
      5. The API gives little variation across occupations. Nearly all occupations are above 80 percent directive.
      6. Task texts are O*NET's English statements.
